Saving · Tip 01

Shop your kitchen first.

Check the fridge, freezer and pantry before buying more.

You avoid buying duplicates and naturally build meals around food that is already paid for.
Storage · Tip 02

Keep older food visible.

Put ingredients that should be used soon where you can see them.

Food hidden at the back of the fridge is easy to forget. Visibility makes it more likely to be used in time.
Planning · Tip 03

Cook once, eat twice.

Prepare an extra portion of rice, vegetables or protein for tomorrow.

One extra portion turns tomorrow's meal into assembly instead of starting from zero.
Cooking · Tip 04

Simple meals are still good meals.

Everyday cooking does not need ten ingredients or complicated techniques.

A small number of well-matched ingredients is easier to cook consistently and usually creates less waste.
Storage · Tip 05

Freeze it before you lose it.

Bread, cooked meals and many ingredients can be frozen before they spoil.

Freezing gives food more time and lets you save useful portions for a day when you do not want to cook.
Cooking · Tip 06

Recipes are guides, not laws.

Learn simple substitutions so you can cook with ingredients already at home.

Flexible cooking makes the recipe fit your kitchen instead of forcing your kitchen to fit the recipe.
Planning · Tip 07

Plan the fragile food first.

Use fresh herbs, leafy vegetables and opened products earlier in the week.

Ingredients with the shortest useful life get priority, while rice, pasta and frozen foods can wait.
Saving · Tip 08

Give leftovers a second job.

Yesterday's vegetables can become soup, fried rice, pasta or a sandwich filling.

Reusing cooked components saves both money and preparation time without repeating the exact same meal.
